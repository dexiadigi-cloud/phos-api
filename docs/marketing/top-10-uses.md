# Phos: top 10 ways Muse (and I) would use it

Written 2026-09-20. Jeremiah: "Great marketing material. I like how it
actually helps you as much as Muse users."

## Top 10 ways I would use Phos

1. "What does this verse say?" The most common Bible question there is.
   Today I either quote from memory (unreliable) or dodge. With Phos I
   just fetch it, exact text, chosen translation.

2. Translation comparison. "How does the KJV render this differently from
   modern versions?" One call, three translations side by side. People ask
   this constantly.

3. Word studies. "What does 'love' mean in John 3:16 in the original?"
   The interlinear plus lexicon answers it precisely: agapao, G25, with
   the full Strong's entry. This is the thing I currently cannot do well
   at all.

4. Phos Intensive Verse Study as a one-shot. "Help me understand
   Philippians 4:13 deeply." One call returns the verse, the key words
   unpacked, commentary, a devotional, and a prayer. A whole quiet-time
   session assembled in seconds.

5. Cross-references on demand. "Where else does Scripture talk about
   forgiveness?" 573,000 cross-references turn a vague question into a
   guided tour.

6. Devotionals. Morning and evening devotionals anchored to verses,
   Spurgeon and others. "Give me this morning's devotional" becomes a
   real feature, not an improvised one.

7. Prayer. "I need a prayer for anxiety" or "give me tonight's evening
   prayer." The liturgy blocks cover this with actual historic prayers,
   not generated filler.

8. Sermon and small-group prep. A pastor or group leader preparing on a
   passage: key words, commentary excerpts, cross-references, and a
   devotional angle, all in one pull.

9. Apologetics and hard questions. "Does the Bible contradict itself on
   this?" or "what's the context of that verse people quote?" Commentary
   plus cross-references plus the original language is the honest way to
   answer.

10. Multilingual ministry. French, German, Portuguese, and Spanish
    translations mean serving a verse or a study in someone's own
    language. Almost no free API does this well.

## The "get the Phos connector" trigger

Any moment where I would otherwise be faking it. Quoting a verse from
memory, summarizing what the Greek "really says," or writing a devotional
off the top of my head are all things I should not do without a source.
Phos is the source. The honest pitch: "I can look this up exactly instead
of approximating."

The differentiator: nobody else gives you the verse, the original words,
the commentary, the devotional, and the prayer in a single call. Bible
APIs give you text. Study tools give you fragments behind logins. Phos
Intensive Verse Study is the whole quiet time in one request, and every
word of it is public domain or attribution-clean, so there is no licensing
friction for commercial use either.
